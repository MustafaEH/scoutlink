# Build With Me — Social Media Launch Plan

Date: 30 October 2026  
Working series name: **Building Egypt's Football Talent Network**  
Arabic series name: to be tested with the audience; use natural Egyptian Arabic, not a literal corporate translation

## 1. Objective

Start building the community, trust, and partnership pipeline before the product is built.

The series should produce three kinds of qualified interest:

1. Players and guardians joining the pilot waitlist.
2. Academies applying to become founding partners.
3. Recruiters contributing requirements and joining the design-partner group.

Views and follower counts are useful distribution signals, but the real measures are verified applications, academy meetings, recruiter interviews, and recruitment briefs.

## 2. Core story

> Egypt has enormous football talent, but too much of it depends on location, personal connections, and being seen at the right moment. We are building a safer, more credible way for Egyptian players to show real football evidence to verified recruiters in Egypt and around the world. Follow the journey and help us build it properly.

The series is not “watch me build an app.” It is “help us build a better path from Egyptian talent to legitimate opportunity.”

## 3. Founder-led format

The founder should be the main face and voice. People will trust a person earlier than a new logo.

Recommended content identity:

- Egyptian Arabic spoken naturally.
- Arabic on-screen captions.
- Accurate English subtitles on every important video.
- Honest, energetic, ambitious, and practical tone.
- Brand account reposts the founder's strongest content.
- LinkedIn version is written in professional English for recruiters and partners.

If the founder does not want to appear on camera, use voiceover with academy visits, pitch footage, product sketches, and interview clips. Founder-on-camera is still the recommended format.

## 4. Channels

### Player and guardian reach

- TikTok
- Instagram Reels
- YouTube Shorts
- Facebook for guardians and local football communities

### Academy and recruiter reach

- LinkedIn founder profile
- LinkedIn company page
- Direct WhatsApp sharing after consent/contact
- Email newsletter: Egypt Talent Brief
- X only if the founder can maintain useful football-industry conversations consistently

### Long-form trust

- One YouTube episode or founder update each month.
- Academy visits, recruiter interviews, pilot lessons, and transparent outcome reports.

Do not create separate original content for every channel. Record one strong source video and adapt the opening, captions, length, and call to action.

## 5. Series structure

Use one recognisable format:

1. **Hook:** one sentence in the first two seconds.
2. **Problem:** show the real football-market issue.
3. **What we are deciding/building:** one decision per episode.
4. **Evidence:** interview, field visit, competitor example, sketch, or user response.
5. **Question:** invite a specific answer from players, parents, academies, or scouts.
6. **Call to action:** one audience and one action only.

Ideal short-video length: approximately 30–60 seconds. Use longer videos only when the story deserves it.

## 6. Content pillars

### 1. The problem

- Why talented Egyptian players are missed.
- The role of geography, connections, video quality, and fragmented scouting.
- What a scout actually needs before watching a player.
- Why a viral skills clip is not the same as match evidence.

### 2. Building the product

- Choosing the first age range.
- Designing the player profile.
- Building the scout discovery feed.
- Likes versus professional-interest signals.
- Why comments are not in the MVP.
- How guardian accounts work.
- What recruiter verification should mean.
- Why every meeting includes a platform representative.

### 3. Building the network

- Academy visits.
- Conversations with coaches.
- Scout and recruiter interviews.
- What international recruiters want from Egyptian players.
- Choosing the first governorates.
- Preparing the first 50-player cohort.

### 4. Football education

- How to film useful match footage.
- What information belongs in a football CV.
- Common fake-scout warning signs.
- Difference between a scout and a licensed football agent.
- What “international opportunity” realistically means by age.
- How trials and recruitment normally work.

### 5. Evidence and progress

- Number of academy interviews—not invented partnerships.
- Number of verified recruiter interviews.
- Product decisions changed by community feedback.
- Before/after profile examples using authorised or fictional data.
- Pilot milestones and honest blockers.
- Meetings, trials, and outcomes only after verification and consent.

### 6. The ambition

- Why Egypt can become a stronger source of globally visible talent.
- Building an Arabic/English evidence standard.
- The long-term dream: the trusted global gateway for Egyptian talent.
- Expansion to girls and more governorates after the first model works.

## 7. First 12 episodes

### Episode 0 — We are building this

Hook:

> How many talented Egyptian footballers never get seen because they don't know the right person?

Story:

- Introduce the problem.
- Explain that the platform will give players verified profiles and videos visible to legitimate recruiters.
- State clearly that it is being built now and the audience will help shape it.

CTA:

> Follow the series and tell me: what is the biggest reason talented players in Egypt get missed?

### Episode 1 — The vision

> We don't want another football video app. We want the place the world checks for Egyptian talent.

Explain the Egyptian supply plus global recruiter ambition.

CTA: players/parents join the interest waitlist.

### Episode 2 — Who gets the first opportunity?

Explain the proposed first cohort: 50 boys aged 12–19, selected through partner academies and evidence readiness.

CTA: ask which governorates should be included first and why.

### Episode 3 — What makes a useful football video?

Compare a flashy isolated trick with a contextual match clip.

CTA: coaches/scouts state what they need to see.

### Episode 4 — How scouts will discover players

Show a paper or simple visual prototype of the vertical feed: applaud, professional interest, shortlist, full profile, meeting.

CTA: recruiters apply for a research interview.

### Episode 5 — Do likes prove talent?

Explain that likes can motivate and help discovery, but verified recruiter interest, relevance, and evidence matter more.

CTA: audience votes between two feed approaches.

### Episode 6 — Why comments are not included

Explain focus, noise reduction, moderation cost, and protection of young players.

CTA: ask what the minimum useful scout action should be.

### Episode 7 — Who counts as a verified recruiter?

Show the proposed verification checklist without exposing security details.

CTA: experienced scouts critique the standard privately or via a form.

### Episode 8 — Why every meeting includes us

Explain guardian trust, scam prevention, accurate outcomes, and practical coordination.

CTA: parents submit their biggest concern about football opportunities.

### Episode 9 — Founding academy partners

Explain what academies receive, what they provide, and why ScoutLink does not claim rights in players.

CTA: academy owners request the founding-partner brief.

### Episode 10 — Egypt to the world, honestly

Explain the age-based pathway: evidence and development for younger players; active global opportunity for eligible older players. State that no transfer or trial is guaranteed.

CTA: international recruiters share which Egyptian profiles they seek.

### Episode 11 — What we learned this month

Publish numbers: interviews, academy meetings, recruiter briefs, waitlist applications, decisions changed, and next goal.

CTA: one specific request based on the biggest current gap.

## 9. Calls to action

Use one CTA per post.

### Player/guardian

- Join the pilot waitlist.
- Tell us your governorate, age, position, and current academy/team.
- Apply only through a guardian for under-18 players.

### Academy

- Request the Founding Academy Partner brief.
- Book a 20-minute founder call.
- Nominate a safeguarding/operations contact.

### Recruiter

- Apply for a research interview.
- Submit a recruitment brief.
- Join the verified design-partner group.

The link-in-bio page should have three separate buttons so audiences do not enter one generic form.

## 11. Capture system before posting

The series needs somewhere to send attention from day one.

Minimum pre-launch funnel:

1. Simple branded landing page.
2. Three paths: Player/Guardian, Academy, Recruiter.
3. Separate short forms and consent language.
4. Automatic acknowledgement explaining that this is a research/pilot waitlist, not acceptance or a promised opportunity.
5. CRM tags by role, governorate, age band, academy, country, and verification status.
6. Interview scheduling link for academy and recruiter prospects.
7. Analytics for source post, completed form, and qualified lead.

Do not collect child identity documents, full birth dates, detailed addresses, or sensitive footage through a generic marketing form.

## 12. Production workflow

### Weekly recording block

- Record three to five episodes in one session.
- Leave space for timely field updates.
- Capture vertical 9:16 at high quality.
- Use a consistent microphone, background, title style, and captions.
- Keep raw files organised by date/episode/consent status.

### Per-video checklist

- One clear idea.
- Hook is understandable without context.
- Arabic captions checked manually.
- English subtitles reviewed by a person.
- No private or unapproved child information.
- Claims can be supported.
- One CTA and trackable link.
- Guardian/media release stored for every identifiable minor.

## 13. What to share and what to keep private

### Share

- problems and learning;
- product decisions;
- sketches and non-sensitive prototypes;
- academy visits with permission;
- aggregated waitlist/interview progress;
- mistakes and changes;
- verified outcomes with consent.

### Keep private

- child documents and contact data;
- unannounced academy/recruiter names;
- contract and negotiation details;
- exact fraud/security checks;
- allegations or rejected-user details;
- confidential competitive information;
- private scout notes;
- footage without clear rights and guardian consent.

Build in public does not mean operate without confidentiality.

## 14. Moderating the social accounts

Even though product comments are excluded, social media comments will exist.

- Hide/delete child sexualisation, bullying, contact requests, scams, and personal information immediately.
- Never allow recruiters to solicit minors in public comments.
- Direct under-18 enquiries to the guardian form.
- Pin an anti-scam notice.
- Do not move a supposed opportunity into private messaging until the person enters the verification process.
- Maintain an escalation record for credible threats or safeguarding concerns.
- Avoid publicly debating whether an individual child is talented.

## 15. Metrics

### Awareness

- qualified reach in Egypt and target recruiter countries;
- profile visits;
- video completion and saves;
- returning viewers;
- English-subtitle engagement.

### Community

- useful responses from players, guardians, coaches, academies, and scouts;
- research interview applications;
- repeat contributors;
- newsletter sign-ups.

### Business

- qualified player/guardian waitlist applications;
- licensed academy leads and meetings;
- verified recruiter leads and completed interviews;
- recruitment briefs received;
- cost/time per qualified relationship.

The series succeeds when it produces trusted participants and better decisions, not merely when an episode goes viral.

## 16. Brand safety rules

- Never promise a contract, trial, or move abroad.
- Never call an unsigned academy/recruiter a partner.
- Never call an ordinary viewer a scout.
- Never use “next Salah” to market a child.
- Never rank children publicly by popularity.
- Never show rejection as humiliation or entertainment.
- Never charge for a place in a social-media feature during the pilot.
- Correct public mistakes visibly and quickly.
- State when metrics are applications, verified users, meetings, trials, or signings.

## 17. Start-now checklist

1. Reserve matching founder/brand handles.
2. Choose a temporary series name even if the company name is not final.
3. Create the three-path waitlist page.
4. Create a simple visual system: two fonts, two main colours, caption template, and end card.
5. Record Episode 0 and three backup episodes before publishing.
6. Prepare the anti-scam pinned post.
7. Prepare the guardian/privacy notice for social submissions.
8. Create the content and lead-tracking sheet.
9. Publish Episode 0 from the founder account and brand account.
10. Personally send it to the first 20 coaches, academy owners, parents, and scouts and ask for honest criticism—not generic shares.
