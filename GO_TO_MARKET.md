# Go-to-Market and Brand Strategy

Date: 30 October 2026  
Market: Egyptian boys aged 12–19 initially; verified recruiter reach worldwide  
Commercial status: free validation pilot; monetisation begins only after demonstrated repeat value

## 1. Strategic ambition

The long-term goal is not merely to operate an Egyptian football app. It is to become the trusted international gateway for Egyptian football talent.

That reputation must be earned in sequence:

1. Build the most credible small collection of Egyptian youth talent.
2. Produce real, safely managed recruiter meetings and trials.
3. Document outcomes honestly.
4. Establish repeat relationships with recruiters and academies.
5. Expand geographic coverage and player volume without weakening evidence or safeguarding.
6. Become the default place international recruitment professionals check for Egyptian talent.

### Recommended category

**Verified Egyptian football talent network**

This is stronger than “scouting app” or “football social network” because it describes both the asset—verified talent—and the network effect we want to build.

### Positioning

> The trusted place to discover Egypt's next football talent.

Alternative player-facing line:

> Your game deserves the right eyes.

Arabic brand lines should be developed with Egyptian players and parents rather than translated literally.

## 2. Two audiences, two promises

### Players and families

Promise:

- Your football story will be presented professionally.
- Your evidence can reach legitimate Egyptian and international recruiters.
- You will not need personal connections to enter the platform.
- Your guardian controls communication if you are under 18.
- The platform attends every meeting.
- Visibility is free and cannot be purchased through ranking boosts during the pilot.

Do not promise:

- a contract;
- a trial;
- a move to Europe;
- selection based on likes;
- that every registered player will be published;
- that international transfer is legally possible for every minor.

### Recruiters, clubs, and academies

Promise:

- A focused source of Egyptian talent rather than an unfiltered upload database.
- Verified identity, age, guardian, and football context.
- Contextual footage alongside highlights.
- Arabic information converted into a consistent English football profile.
- Fast filters, feed discovery, shortlists, and human-supported meetings.
- One accountable contact in Egypt.
- Clear availability and last-confirmed dates.

Do not claim that ScoutLink guarantees player quality, future performance, contractual availability, work permits, or transfer approval.

## 3. International opportunity without false hope

The “global opportunity” message must change by age.

### Ages 12–15 — Build the evidence

- Create a credible long-term football record.
- Receive Egyptian academy/club visibility.
- Allow approved international recruiters to follow development where lawful.
- Focus marketing on development and being properly seen, not moving abroad.

### Ages 16–17 — Prepare the pathway

- Stronger evidence, full-match context, and verified history.
- Domestic trials and appropriate recruiter meetings.
- International monitoring and preparation only within applicable FIFA/EFA rules.
- Representation only when the legal age/window and agent requirements permit it.

### Ages 18–19 — Activate global opportunity

- Domestic and international recruiter discovery.
- Trial and meeting coordination.
- Licensed representation if voluntarily appointed.
- Contract, transfer, visa, work-permit, and registration processes handled through the proper professional channels.

FIFA generally prohibits international transfers of under-18 players except for narrowly defined exceptions. Marketing must never suggest that a child profile is a normal route to an immediate European transfer.

## 4. Market-entry model

The marketplace should not open nationally on day one. Quality density matters more than registrations.

### Pilot composition

- 50 boys aged 12–19.
- 3–5 licensed, reputable academy/community partners.
- One or two Egyptian governorates initially.
- A balanced position/age mix rather than 50 attacking players.
- 10–15 verified recruiters with real needs.
- Egyptian recruiters plus a small international group.

### Supply acquisition mix

- 70% from academy and club partnerships.
- 20% from observed tournaments, leagues, and showcase days.
- 10% from guardian/player nominations and trusted referrals.

Avoid uncontrolled open registration during the pilot. A waitlist can collect interest, but only selected and verified players become discoverable.

### Demand acquisition principle

Recruit demand before scaling supply. Each recruiter should bring at least one real football requirement or commit to structured discovery sessions.

## 5. Academy partnership strategy

Academies are not merely acquisition channels. They are verification partners, content partners, and trusted access points to families.

The Egyptian Football Association's current academy framework requires academy licensing and emphasises player rights, qualified coaching, child safeguarding, medical care, and oversight. ScoutLink should prioritise licensed partners and align the partnership checklist with those requirements.

### Academy value proposition

During the pilot, partner academies receive free:

- verified academy page and badge;
- digital player roster;
- professional player-profile templates;
- one organised profile/video collection day;
- academy-validated history and coach reference workflow;
- recruiter enquiry dashboard;
- co-branded player CVs;
- notification when one of their players receives qualified interest;
- aggregate information about profile completeness, views, and opportunities;
- global partner visibility in Arabic and English;
- safeguarding and opportunity records.

### What ScoutLink asks from an academy

- current EFA/appropriate operating licence evidence;
- named academy owner/manager and safeguarding contact;
- permission to verify team membership and football history;
- guardian introductions and consent support;
- access to a suitable filming location or match footage;
- honest coach evaluations and availability information;
- immediate notification when a player leaves or changes status;
- compliance with the meeting and child-protection rules;
- permission to use approved academy name/logo and agreed footage.

### The academy's biggest fear: losing control of players

The agreement should state:

- the academy remains clearly attributed on the player profile;
- ScoutLink will not claim registration or economic rights in the player;
- recruiters cannot secretly contact a minor;
- the academy is informed of a qualified opportunity where the guardian and applicable rules permit;
- player/guardian rights and lawful career choices are not restricted;
- academy contact information is not handed to competitors without permission;
- no ScoutLink marketing may falsely imply academy endorsement beyond the signed partnership.

### Partnership tiers during the free pilot

- **Founding Academy Partner:** supplies 10–20 qualified players, validates records, participates in monthly review.
- **Talent Source Partner:** supplies a smaller cohort or tournament access.
- **Video/Evidence Partner:** helps capture full matches or contextual footage.

No paid “official partner” badge. Partnership status reflects documented participation and verification.

## 6. Recruiter acquisition strategy

### Recruiter segments in order

1. Egyptian club and academy recruitment staff.
2. Egyptian independent scouts with verifiable placement history.
3. Gulf club/academy recruiters and licensed agencies.
4. North African recruiters and clubs.
5. European recruitment staff and agencies already active in Africa/MENA.
6. Wider global recruitment network after evidence of repeat outcomes.

Europe should be a deliberate target, not the only promise. Gulf, North African, other African, Asian, and domestic Egyptian opportunities may be more immediately practical for particular players.

### Recruitment approach

Do not begin with mass advertising to scouts. Use founder-led outbound:

- build a list of 100 named recruitment professionals;
- prioritise people whose role and organisation can be verified;
- request a 20-minute discovery interview, not a sales call;
- collect their position/age/market requirements;
- prepare a small private shortlist using real pilot players;
- invite the best 10–15 to a free design-partner programme;
- hold monthly recruiter councils to review evidence and workflow.

### Recruiter pilot offer

> Tell us what profile you need. We will show you a small, verified shortlist of Egyptian players with contextual evidence and coordinate every next step locally.

Free access requires:

- a real recruitment brief;
- professional verification;
- structured feedback on relevance and evidence quality;
- compliance with guardian and meeting rules;
- accurate opportunity updates;
- two product-research sessions during the pilot.

## 7. Global reputation strategy

Global credibility will not come from claiming to be “the number one platform.” It comes from consistent evidence and professional signals.

### Credibility assets

1. English and Arabic player profiles using consistent football terminology.
2. Transparent explanation of player and recruiter verification.
3. Named academy and recruiter partners, with permission.
4. A small international recruitment advisory group.
5. Published aggregate outcomes, including failures and conversion rates.
6. High-quality contextual footage rather than only viral highlights.
7. Clear child-safeguarding and meeting policy.
8. Professional response time and one accountable local coordinator.
9. References from recruiters who returned and used the service again.
10. Eventually, licensed agent and legal/registration capability for eligible players.

### Egypt Talent Brief

Publish a monthly English-language professional briefing for recruiters:

- available position/age profiles;
- newly verified players;
- one academy spotlight;
- selected clips with context;
- upcoming tournament/match coverage;
- aggregate trends without exposing child data;
- instructions for submitting a recruitment brief.

This should be an email/LinkedIn document aimed at football professionals, not a consumer newsletter.

### International advisory network

Recruit 5–8 respected professionals across Egypt, the Gulf, North Africa, and Europe. They do not receive automatic access to children; they complete the same verification. Their role is to:

- review evidence standards;
- explain market needs;
- introduce legitimate demand partners;
- critique the product quarterly;
- lend credibility only after genuine participation.

Avoid decorative “ambassadors” who provide a photo but no market access or expertise.

## 8. Player and parent acquisition

The founder-led **Build With Me** content series begins before product development and acts as the public narrative for the pilot. Its detailed editorial, safety, CTA, and 30-day publishing plan is defined in `BUILD_WITH_ME_SERIES.md`.

### Main channels

- academy information sessions;
- tournament and match filming days;
- coach referrals;
- guardian WhatsApp information groups operated with strict privacy rules;
- Instagram, TikTok, Facebook, and YouTube storytelling;
- player waitlist and academy referral codes;
- local sports media after real outcomes exist.

### Content pillars

1. **Player evidence:** guardian-approved clips showing match context, not just tricks.
2. **Education:** how scouting works, what scouts look for, and how to build a credible profile.
3. **Safety:** how to recognise fake scouts and why meetings include the platform.
4. **Development:** coach-led advice without promising selection.
5. **Opportunity stories:** verified meetings, trials, and registrations with clear wording.
6. **Academy stories:** facilities, coaching philosophy, and player development.
7. **Behind the process:** verification, filming, translation, and recruiter matching.

### Content rules

- Guardian approval for every minor appearance.
- No school, home, precise schedule, phone, or private location information.
- No “next Mohamed Salah” headlines.
- No humiliating rejections or public comparison of children.
- No claim that likes equal professional potential.
- No manufactured scout testimonials.
- Clearly distinguish a profile view, meeting, trial invitation, trial attendance, registration, and contract.

## 9. The discovery feed as marketing

The feed serves players and recruiters, but public marketing should not expose detailed minor profiles.

### Public layer

- Guardian-approved teaser clips where appropriate.
- Player display name or limited identity according to age/privacy settings.
- General position/age band/governorate.
- Academy attribution with permission.
- Call to action for a recruiter to complete verification.

### Verified marketplace layer

- Full player identity where authorised.
- Detailed football history.
- Contextual/full-match evidence.
- Guardian/representation status.
- Availability and opportunity controls.
- Professional-interest and meeting actions.

Likes/applause can create motivation and social proof, but recruiter matching, verified professional interest, and evidence quality determine opportunity distribution.

## 10. Launch campaign

### Phase 0 — credibility before launch

Duration: 4–6 weeks.

- Sign 3 founding academy partners.
- Verify the first 50 players and produce complete profiles.
- Recruit 10–15 design-partner recruiters with real briefs.
- Publish verification, safeguarding, and meeting policies.
- Produce the first English Egypt Talent Brief.
- Prepare founder, academy, guardian, and recruiter stories.

Do not publicly launch an empty feed.

### Phase 1 — controlled pilot launch

Duration: 8–12 weeks.

- Private marketplace opens to verified recruiters.
- Public player/parent waitlist opens.
- Publish weekly player-evidence and education content.
- Run recruiter discovery sessions.
- Attend and document every meeting.
- Release only verified aggregate milestones.

Suggested launch message:

> Fifty Egyptian players. Professionally presented. Safely connected to verified football recruiters worldwide.

### Phase 2 — prove outcomes

Months 4–6.

- Publish a transparent pilot outcome report.
- Expand to 150–250 players only if recruiter activity supports it.
- Add new academies by governorate.
- Turn successful design partners into references.
- Hold the first invitation-only online Egyptian Talent Review for verified professionals.
- Test willingness to pay without activating broad fees prematurely.

### Phase 3 — national and international brand building

Months 6–18, conditional on proof.

- Expand academy coverage across Egypt.
- Establish recurring international recruiter councils.
- Cover selected tournaments and academy leagues.
- Produce position-specific talent reports.
- Form club/agency partnerships in chosen destination markets.
- Introduce paid professional plans only when value and unit economics are demonstrated.
- Evaluate a physical showcase only after ordinary meetings/trials work reliably.

## 11. Channel strategy

| Audience | Primary channels | Content/action |
|---|---|---|
| Players 12–19 | TikTok, Instagram, YouTube, academy sessions | Clips, education, waitlist, profile stories |
| Guardians | Academy meetings, Facebook, WhatsApp, website | Safety, consent, realistic pathways, FAQs |
| Egyptian academies | Founder outreach, site visits, referrals, EFA ecosystem | Partnership deck, free pilot roster, filming day |
| Egyptian scouts/clubs | Direct outreach, football network, LinkedIn, matches | Recruitment briefs, private shortlists, demos |
| International recruiters | LinkedIn, email, referrals, professional events | English Egypt Talent Brief, curated evidence, brief intake |
| Football media | Direct relationships and press kits | Verified outcomes, academy partnerships, market insight |

Paid consumer advertising should be minimal until recruiter demand is active. Large player acquisition without opportunities will damage trust.

## 12. Partnership pipeline

Track academy and recruiter relationships like sales opportunities.

### Academy pipeline stages

Target → Contacted → Meeting → Licence/safeguarding review → Pilot proposal → Agreement → Player selection → Profiles live → Active partner → Renewal/expansion

### Recruiter pipeline stages

Target → Identity researched → Interview → Verification → Brief received → First shortlist → Active review → Meeting requested → Repeat use → Reference/paid conversion candidate

Required CRM fields include owner, last contact, next action, evidence received, risks, expected player/brief volume, and relationship source.

## 13. Metrics

### Supply quality

- signed and active academy partners;
- selected versus recruiter-ready players;
- evidence-complete profiles;
- academy-validated records;
- position, age, and governorate coverage;
- time and cost to produce one recruiter-ready profile.

### Demand quality

- verified recruiters by country and type;
- recruiters submitting a real brief;
- monthly active recruiters;
- repeat recruiters;
- qualified feed views and profile completions;
- briefs that produce at least three credible matches.

### Opportunity outcomes

- professional interest signals;
- shortlists;
- meeting requests and completed meetings;
- trial invitations and attended trials;
- academy/club registrations;
- professional contracts and registered transfers;
- time between each stage;
- outcome confirmation rate.

### Brand and trust

- academy/recruiter referrals;
- recruiter newsletter engagement;
- international inbound enquiries;
- waitlist-to-approved conversion;
- guardian satisfaction;
- report and safeguarding incident rate;
- percentage of public claims backed by evidence.

Follower and view counts are supporting marketing metrics, not proof of marketplace success.

## 14. Team responsibilities during the pilot

At minimum, named owners are required for:

- academy partnerships;
- player/guardian onboarding;
- filming and profile production;
- recruiter partnerships and verification;
- safeguarding;
- meeting coordination;
- English/Arabic content;
- outcome follow-up and analytics.

One founder may initially cover multiple roles, but safeguarding and meeting coverage need backup people. Attending every meeting is a meaningful operating cost and must be measured from day one.

## 15. Marketing budget principles

During the free pilot, spend on proof rather than reach:

Priority spend:

1. footage and profile quality;
2. academy visits and guardian sessions;
3. recruiter verification and outreach;
4. English/Arabic content production;
5. meeting attendance and safeguarding;
6. simple CRM and analytics.

Delay:

- broad paid player-acquisition campaigns;
- celebrity ambassadors;
- large launch events;
- expensive sponsorships;
- paid profile promotion;
- international PR without verified outcomes.

## 16. Strategic risks

| Risk | Marketing consequence | Response |
|---|---|---|
| Too many players, too few recruiters | Families lose faith | Gate player growth to active demand |
| “Europe” becomes an implied promise | Legal/reputation harm | Age-specific messaging and explicit no-guarantee language |
| Academies fear player poaching | Partnerships fail | Attribution, transparent opportunity flow, no economic-rights claim |
| Viral clips outrank credible players | Product becomes entertainment | Weighted professional signals and exploration distribution |
| Fake recruiter causes harm | Brand could collapse | Strong verification, no direct minor contact, platform at every meeting |
| No real outcomes | Brand becomes another profile app | Curated briefs, high-touch matching, public outcome definitions |
| Global claim lacks global demand | Credibility loss | Name countries/partners only when verified and active |
| Operational service does not scale | Poor response and meetings | Measure workload, standardise, expand slowly |

## 17. First 30-day action plan

1. Choose the first one or two governorates.
2. Create a list of 30 licensed academy prospects.
3. Interview ten academy owners/coaches about fears, incentives, and data ownership.
4. Build the one-page academy partnership offer.
5. Create a list of 100 named recruiter prospects across Egypt and selected international markets.
6. Interview 15 recruiters and collect at least three real briefs.
7. Appoint the safeguarding lead and meeting-coverage backup.
8. Finalise player evidence and profile acceptance standards.
9. Produce a sample Arabic/English player profile using authorised test data.
10. Create the recruiter-verification and academy-verification checklists.
11. Build a simple CRM for academy and recruiter pipelines.
12. Recruit the first three founding academy partners before opening the player waitlist publicly.
