# Competitive Research — Egyptian Youth Football Talent Platform

Date: 30 October 2026  
Scope: Egypt-first player discovery, youth portfolios, recruiter access, meetings, and later representation  
Research basis: publicly available product pages, app-store listings, terms, and official company materials. Product claims and self-reported metrics have not been independently audited.

## 1. Executive conclusion

The market does not lack football profile or highlight-video apps. Egypt already has multiple products promising that players can upload clips and connect with scouts. Globally, mature platforms provide full-match video, data, trials, player networks, and professional transfer workflows.

ScoutLink may use a social-style highlight feed because it is an efficient discovery interaction, but it should not position the feed itself as the product. That interaction is crowded and easy to copy; verification, contextual evidence, safe meetings, and outcomes remain the differentiation.

The strongest available position is:

> **Egypt's verified talent desk: trusted player evidence, qualified global recruiter access, guardian-controlled communication, and a platform representative at every meeting.**

The product moat should come from operations and trust rather than the profile UI:

- verified player age, guardian, football history, and media context;
- verified recruiter identity and professional purpose;
- evidence beyond edited highlights;
- demand-led matching to real recruitment briefs;
- no direct recruiter-to-child contact;
- every meeting coordinated and attended by the platform;
- documented outcomes from view to meeting, trial, registration, or contract;
- a compliant path to licensed representation for eligible players.

## 4. Gaps worth filling

### Gap 1 — trust is claimed, but rarely explained

Many platforms say they have verified scouts or real clubs, but the public experience often does not explain the checks, badge meaning, expiry, or consequences of misconduct.

ScoutLink opportunity:

- publish the recruiter verification standard;
- show separate badges for identity, organisation, employment, and agent licence;
- show last verification date;
- route every minor interaction through a guardian and the platform;
- maintain visible report and appeal processes.

### Gap 2 — highlights are abundant; reliable context is scarce

A player can upload their best ten seconds anywhere. Recruiters need to know whether the footage is current, who the player is, the match level, the opposition, and what happened outside the highlight.

ScoutLink opportunity:

- require match/date/opponent/competition/shirt metadata;
- mark edited highlight versus continuous passage versus full match;
- require player identification at clip start;
- obtain academy/coach validation where possible;
- show source and last-confirmed date;
- require at least one longer contextual sequence for a profile to become “recruiter ready.”

### Gap 3 — most products optimise views, not qualified demand

Feeds and rankings generate engagement but may reward editing, popularity, paid boosts, or an existing audience rather than football fit.

ScoutLink opportunity:

- recruiters submit structured briefs: position, age band, foot, location, level, passport/eligibility, dates, and opportunity type;
- platform staff produce a small evidence-backed shortlist;
- players are matched to real needs as well as receiving exploration distribution in the feed;
- applause may exist, but no paid ranking and no follower-count competition;
- verified recruiter interest and shortlist actions carry more weight than general likes.

### Gap 4 — discovery stops before the risky part

Direct chat or WhatsApp unlock moves the most sensitive activity outside the product precisely when a child or family needs protection.

ScoutLink opportunity:

- structured meeting request rather than free direct messaging;
- guardian approval;
- verified attendee list and purpose;
- platform representative at every meeting;
- check-in/check-out and post-meeting follow-up;
- recorded path from meeting to trial and later outcome.

### Gap 5 — Egypt lacks a trusted bridge to international recruiters

Global products focus on already-covered leagues, partner academies, large countries, or entertainment-scale acquisition. Egyptian grassroots and smaller academies are not consistently captured in professional-quality formats.

ScoutLink opportunity:

- Arabic/English profiles and human translation of important information;
- consistent football taxonomy and evidence format familiar to international scouts;
- verified Egyptian academy/coach references;
- guardian-supported scheduling across time zones;
- later, eligibility/passport and permitted-transfer context without promising relocation.

### Gap 6 — outcomes are opaque

Views, downloads, registered players, and scout counts do not tell families whether meaningful opportunities occurred.

ScoutLink opportunity:

- report verified funnel outcomes separately: qualified view, shortlist, meeting, trial invitation, trial attended, club/academy registration, professional contract, registered transfer;
- require evidence or confirmation from both sides for high-value outcomes;
- publish aggregate conversion and safety metrics once sample sizes are meaningful.

## 5. Recommended strategic position

### Positioning statement

For Egyptian boys aged 12–19 who have professional potential but lack access to trusted recruitment networks, ScoutLink is a verified talent and opportunity service that turns credible football evidence into safely managed meetings with legitimate recruiters worldwide. Unlike highlight-feed apps, ScoutLink verifies the people and evidence, protects guardians and players, and remains involved until the opportunity outcome is known.

### What ScoutLink is not

- Not TikTok for football.
- Not an open social network.
- Not a pay-to-rank service.
- Not a promise of professional contracts or overseas transfers.
- Not an AI “potential score.”
- Not a bidding platform for children.
- Not an unlicensed football agency.

### Defensible assets to build

1. Trusted relationships with Egyptian academies, coaches, tournaments, and families.
2. A verified network of recruiters with known professional histories.
3. Longitudinal, contextual evidence on players who are absent from major databases.
4. A documented opportunity/outcome graph showing which recruiter needs lead to real trials and registrations.
5. Safeguarding operations and a reputation for handling youth meetings responsibly.
6. Licensed representation capability for eligible players, kept separate from discovery access.

## 6. MVP changes recommended from the research

### Build

- Guardian-controlled onboarding for under-18 players.
- Vertical highlight feed with applause, professional-interest, shortlist, pass, and profile actions.
- Transparent multi-signal recommendations with a new-player exploration quota.
- Human-reviewed player profile and footage.
- Structured recruiter brief and search.
- Evidence labels and profile readiness checklist.
- Recruiter verification with visible badge definitions.
- Staff-curated shortlist workflow.
- Meeting request, guardian approval, attendee verification, platform attendance, and outcome recording.
- Arabic/English interface and profile fields.
- Internal operations console for verification, matching, meetings, reports, and follow-ups.
- Funnel analytics from recruiter brief to trial.

### Do not build in the MVP

- A feed ranked mainly by raw likes, views, or existing audience size.
- Comments or follower-count competition.
- Paid boosts or “featured player” placement.
- AI player score or automated potential prediction.
- Direct WhatsApp/contact unlock.
- Unrestricted recruiter-player chat.
- Automated full-match event analysis.
- Transfer bidding or in-product negotiation.
- Native apps before the responsive web pilot proves repeat use.

### Evidence standard for the first 50 players

Each recruiter-ready profile should contain:

- verified player age and guardian where required;
- current team/academy or independent status;
- position, foot, height, location at governorate level, and football history;
- one short introduction;
- one highlight reel, clearly labelled as edited;
- at least three contextual clips with match metadata;
- ideally one continuous 5–10 minute sequence or full-match link;
- coach/academy reference when available;
- availability and last-confirmed date;
- Arabic data plus an English recruiter summary.

## 7. Free pilot strategy

The first validation period should be free on all sides, but access should remain invitation-based and obligations should be explicit.

### Pilot cohort

- 50 Egyptian boys aged 12–19.
- 3–5 academy/community partners.
- 10–15 verified recruiters, including Egyptian and international participants.
- 8–12 weeks of active matching after profiles become recruiter-ready.

### Recruiter design-partner agreement

Free access in exchange for:

- a real recruitment brief;
- at least two review sessions per month;
- reasons for shortlist/rejection using structured categories;
- outcome updates after meetings;
- compliance with safeguarding and meeting rules;
- no contact bypass or unauthorised data export.

### Player/guardian pilot promise

- No fee.
- No paid visibility advantage.
- No guarantee of a meeting, trial, or contract.
- Clear control over profile publication and withdrawal.
- Platform presence at every meeting.
- Honest feedback when recruiters provide it.

### Evidence required before monetisation

Recommended minimum gate:

- 10 qualified meetings completed;
- 3 verified trial invitations;
- 5 recruiters return and actively use the product in a second month;
- at least 60% of recruiter briefs produce a credible shortlist;
- median response/scheduling time within the agreed target;
- no unresolved high-severity safeguarding case;
- at least 5 recruiters explicitly state a willingness to pay and give a plausible price range.

Do not begin charging merely because the pilot period ended. Charge when repeat value is demonstrated.

## 8. Research-backed risks

### Competitor crowding

At least three current Egyptian products publicly offer some combination of player profiles, videos, feeds, search, shortlists, and direct connection. A generic launch will be indistinguishable.

Mitigation: lead with the managed service and proof standard, not feature quantity.

### Chicken-and-egg liquidity

ScoutMena's publicly displayed numbers—86 talents and three scouts when researched—illustrate that recruiting demand may be harder to acquire than player supply.

Mitigation: recruit verified demand and collect real briefs before onboarding players at scale.

### Self-reported footage quality

Highlight platforms risk exaggerated or non-comparable evidence.

Mitigation: contextual footage, metadata, human review, academy confirmation, and no universal score.

### High operational cost

Attending every meeting, reviewing videos, translating profiles, and verifying recruiters make this a service-heavy business.

Mitigation: restrict the pilot, measure staff minutes per player/meeting, standardise checklists, and price the later professional plan around actual cost.

### Trust versus growth

Direct contact and open feeds can grow faster than guarded workflows.

Mitigation: accept slower growth. The value proposition is trustworthy access to children and young adults, where safety and evidence matter more than download totals.

## 9. Immediate validation work

1. Secret-shop Vizrr, The Scout, Scout Egypt, ScoutMena, and Scout for Sports Investment using founder-owned test accounts without submitting false identity information.
2. Interview ten Egyptian scouts/coaches and five international recruiters who cover Africa/MENA.
3. Ask each recruiter to rank the current proposed player fields and evidence standard.
4. Obtain three real recruitment briefs before selecting the 50-player cohort.
5. Test whether recruiters prefer database search, staff-curated shortlists, or both.
6. Measure how much contextual video is necessary before a recruiter agrees to a meeting.
7. Ask what verified evidence would make an Egyptian grassroots player credible.
8. Record willingness to pay only after demonstrating a shortlist or meeting.
9. Confirm competitor recruiter-verification and child-contact flows through legitimate product use.
10. Revisit this report quarterly; several Egyptian competitors are new and changing quickly.
