# Founder Partnership Framework

Date: 30 October 2026  
Context: two Egyptian founders—one technical/product founder and one football-domain founder who originated the idea and may also be a player/user

This is a commercial framework for discussion, not a substitute for advice from an Egyptian startup lawyer, tax adviser, or accountant.

## 1. Core principle

Equity should reflect future contribution, risk, commitment, and responsibility—not the idea alone and not the number of lines of code.

Both halves of this company are difficult:

- Building a secure product and operating its technology.
- Creating trusted academy supply, verified recruiter demand, safeguarding operations, and real football outcomes.

A strong nontechnical co-founder can deserve equal or near-equal equity if they actually own and deliver the second half. A person who contributes only the original concept, occasional feedback, or their own player profile should receive much less.

## 2. Recommended sequence

### Stage 1 — founder trial, 8 weeks

Do not immediately make an irreversible equity split. Sign a short pre-incorporation founder memorandum covering confidentiality, work ownership, expenses, decision-making, and the intention to negotiate equity after the trial.

During the trial:

- no founder salary;
- neither founder may promise equity, fees, trials, transfers, or partnerships to third parties;
- expenses require written approval and are recorded;
- work created for the venture is documented and later assigned to the company;
- both founders log meaningful weekly commitments and outcomes;
- hold one founder review every week.

### Stage 2 — equity decision

At the end of eight weeks, decide whether:

1. both continue as co-founders;
2. one becomes an adviser/ambassador rather than a founder;
3. the working relationship ends cleanly;
4. the trial is extended for a defined reason and date.

### Stage 3 — incorporation before sensitive pilot operations

Interviews and a minimal waitlist can begin before incorporation with careful data minimisation. Incorporate and put contracts, IP, privacy roles, and liability under the company before collecting real child profiles/documents, signing academy partnerships, or operating recruiter meetings.

GAFI currently offers online incorporation for several structures, including limited liability companies. An Egyptian lawyer/accountant should confirm whether an LLC or another structure best fits investment, founder vesting, regulated football activity, and tax requirements.

## 3. Proposed founder roles

Titles should follow actual responsibility. Do not make someone CEO merely because they had the original idea.

### Technical/Product Founder — proposed CTO or CEO/CTO initially

Owns:

- product strategy and requirements;
- UX and technical design;
- software development and quality;
- infrastructure, security, privacy implementation, and analytics;
- technical vendors and costs;
- release plan and product operations;
- technical hiring and engineering standards;
- translating research into a usable product.

Trial deliverables:

- product specification and prioritised MVP backlog;
- prototype of the key player and recruiter journeys;
- simple three-path landing/waitlist experience;
- CRM/data structure for early research;
- implementation plan and realistic cost estimate;
- privacy/security design for the pilot;
- weekly product demos.

### Football/Business Founder — proposed CEO, COO, or Head of Football depending capability

Owns:

- academy and coach relationships;
- player/guardian research and onboarding operations;
- recruiter discovery, interviews, and partnerships;
- football evidence and profile standards;
- field operations, filming access, and meetings;
- safeguarding implementation with qualified support;
- founder-led or co-led content and community;
- market feedback and commercial validation;
- tracking real-world outcomes.

Trial deliverables:

- 20 player/guardian interviews;
- 10 academy/coach interviews;
- 15 recruiter/scout interviews, including at least three outside Egypt if possible;
- at least three real recruitment briefs;
- three academy pilot letters of intent or documented next-step commitments;
- a credible pipeline for the first 50 players;
- participation in at least two social posts per week or an agreed production role;
- documented safeguarding and meeting-operation research;
- weekly CRM updates and founder review.

If the friend is also a player, that experience is valuable for research and storytelling, but their founder contribution must extend beyond being the first user. Their profile must not receive hidden ranking or access advantages.

## 4. Equity ranges

These are negotiation ranges, not legal rules.

| Situation after the trial | Reasonable discussion range |
|---|---|
| Both full-time or similarly committed; each owns an essential half and consistently delivers | 50/50 to 55/45 |
| Technical founder is full-time; business founder is serious but materially part-time | 60/40 to 70/30 |
| Business founder mainly contributed the idea, personal story, and occasional introductions | 80/20 to 90/10, or adviser equity instead |
| Friend is primarily an ambassador/test player rather than company builder | Approximately 1–5% adviser/ambassador grant, usually vested and milestone-based |

### Recommended default if the friend proves the role

**55% technical/product founder and 45% football/business founder**, both subject to the same vesting.

Why this can work:

- recognises that the current product/specification/engineering burden is concentrated with the technical founder;
- still treats the football/business founder as a genuine co-founder, not an employee;
- avoids an automatic 50/50 voting deadlock;
- can be adjusted toward 50/50 if commitment, cash risk, leadership, and results are genuinely equal.

Do not use a large permanent split to motivate future work. Vesting provides that motivation safely.

## 5. Founder vesting

Recommended commercial structure:

- four-year vesting;
- one-year cliff;
- monthly vesting over the remaining 36 months;
- vesting start date may credit a short, specifically agreed period of meaningful pre-incorporation work;
- all founders follow the same basic rule;
- unvested equity returns to the company/other agreed holder when a founder leaves;
- acceleration only for carefully defined acquisition/termination events, not ordinary resignation.

Example: if a founder leaves after eight months, none of the founder equity is vested. At twelve months, 25% vests; the remainder vests monthly.

Egyptian company and tax law may require vesting to be implemented through repurchase rights, call options, conditional transfers, or another structure. Do not copy a US template without local counsel.

## 6. Decision rights

### Technical founder decides within approved plan

- architecture and engineering practices;
- security implementation;
- technical vendors within budget;
- release safety and incident response;
- technical hiring recommendations.

### Football/business founder decides within approved plan

- academy/recruiter outreach sequencing;
- football profile and evidence recommendations;
- field interview scheduling;
- routine partnership management;
- content participation and football-community operations.

### Both founders must approve

- equity or option issuance;
- adding/removing a founder;
- incorporation changes;
- taking investment or debt;
- spending above an agreed threshold;
- founder salaries;
- hiring senior staff;
- entering a regulated agency/representation business;
- charging players or guardians;
- major partnership exclusivity;
- promises involving minors, trials, transfers, or guaranteed outcomes;
- sale, merger, shutdown, or material IP transfer;
- changing the core business model.

Safety and legal compliance override ordinary domain authority. Either founder can pause an unsafe minor interaction, data exposure, or misleading public claim pending review.

## 7. Deadlock handling

Even with 55/45 ownership, do not let ordinary share percentage silently determine every operating dispute.

Recommended sequence:

1. Write each position, evidence, cost, and reversible test.
2. Run a time-boxed experiment where possible.
3. Seek a mutually selected adviser with relevant expertise.
4. Use mediation for major unresolved disputes.
5. Apply a lawyer-drafted buyout or separation mechanism only as a last resort.

Avoid aggressive shotgun/buy-sell clauses until both founders have legal advice and comparable access to capital; otherwise the richer founder gains an unfair advantage.

## 8. Intellectual property and accounts

The company should ultimately own:

- source code and infrastructure configuration;
- product designs and specifications;
- brand, domain, and social accounts;
- original content created for the venture;
- databases and internal taxonomies;
- contracts, research records, and operating playbooks;
- licensed rights to player/academy media as defined by consent and agreements.

Before incorporation, maintain a schedule of founder-created work. On incorporation, both founders sign written IP assignments to the company.

Rules:

- source code lives in a company-controlled repository with role-based access;
- domains, hosting, email, analytics, and social accounts use company-controlled credentials;
- no founder holds the only recovery method;
- personal pre-existing IP must be listed and either excluded or licensed explicitly;
- open-source and third-party licences are tracked;
- player data is not founder property and cannot be taken after departure.

## 9. Cash, expenses, and salary

- Record each founder's cash contribution separately from equity.
- Decide in writing whether founder cash is a company loan, reimbursable expense, or equity investment.
- Require receipts and approval above a small threshold.
- No founder salary during the initial free pilot unless both agree and the company can afford it.
- When salaries begin, base them on role, time, and affordability—not necessarily identical equity.
- Do not make one founder's unpaid labour invisible; record time and deliverables even though hours alone do not determine equity.

## 10. Founder conduct and conflicts

Both founders should disclose:

- work, school, playing, agency, academy, or club commitments;
- financial interests in academies, agents, clubs, or vendors;
- family relationships affecting selection or contracting;
- other products or side businesses;
- any restriction imposed by an employer, club, academy, or player contract.

The agreement should include:

- confidentiality;
- truthful public statements;
- no unauthorised promise of trials or contracts;
- no preferential ranking for founders, relatives, or friends;
- safeguarding duties and immediate reporting;
- anti-harassment and anti-exploitation rules;
- narrow, locally enforceable non-solicitation/conflict terms rather than an unnecessarily broad non-compete;
- return of credentials, records, and equipment on departure.

Serious safeguarding misconduct, fraud, theft, deliberate data misuse, or impersonating a licensed agent should trigger immediate suspension of authority while legal steps are followed.

## 11. Leaving the company

Agree in advance:

- what happens to unvested equity;
- treatment of vested equity;
- resignation notice;
- handover obligations;
- removal from accounts and legal authority;
- repayment of valid founder loans;
- rights to be credited for historical work;
- confidentiality and data obligations after departure;
- how the company may buy vested shares and how value is determined;
- death, incapacity, or long-term inability to contribute.

A friendship is better protected by deciding this while the relationship is healthy.

## 12. Adviser and employee pool

Plan for a future employee/adviser option pool, commonly discussed in the range of 10–15% before institutional fundraising, but do not issue casual equity promises now.

Any pool generally dilutes both founders proportionally unless an investment agreement says otherwise. Grants should have written role, vesting, milestones where appropriate, and company approval.

## 13. Eight-week founder trial scorecard

Review every Friday.

| Area | Technical/product founder | Football/business founder |
|---|---|---|
| Customer research | Join key interviews; translate findings into product decisions | Schedule/lead field interviews and maintain CRM |
| Supply | Design onboarding/evidence workflow | Three academy commitments and 50-player pipeline |
| Demand | Build recruiter brief/feed prototype | 15 recruiter interviews and three real briefs |
| Product | Prototype, backlog, architecture, landing funnel | Validate terminology, workflow, and evidence standard |
| Marketing | Build capture system and analytics | Co-create/appear/distribute in Build With Me series |
| Trust | Privacy/security design | Safeguarding and meeting-operation research |
| Operations | Tooling and data model | Field logistics, guardian/academy coordination |
| Commitment | Agreed weekly time and demos | Agreed weekly time and documented outreach |

At weeks 4 and 8, each founder privately scores:

- reliability;
- time commitment;
- quality of work;
- ownership without reminders;
- ability to learn;
- honesty and judgment;
- comfort handling difficult conversations;
- expected two-year contribution;
- personal financial/career risk.

Discuss differences with evidence, not emotion.

## 14. Pre-incorporation founder memorandum checklist

A lawyer-drafted short memorandum should cover:

1. Working project name and purpose.
2. Founder names and temporary roles.
3. Eight-week trial dates and weekly commitment.
4. Deliverables and scorecard.
5. No final equity promised until the review date.
6. Confidentiality.
7. Ownership and later assignment of work product.
8. Treatment of pre-existing IP.
9. Expense approval and recording.
10. Authority limits and prohibited promises.
11. Data and safeguarding rules.
12. Account/repository access.
13. Decision-making during the trial.
14. Exit and deletion/return of confidential material.
15. Governing law and dispute process.

## 15. Incorporation note for Egypt

GAFI provides electronic incorporation services and lists limited liability companies among the supported structures. An LLC may be a practical early-stage option for two founders, but the correct form depends on investment plans, capital, governance, employee equity, regulated football services, and tax treatment.

Before incorporation, ask Egyptian counsel/accounting advisers specifically about:

- implementing founder vesting and repurchase rights;
- share transfer restrictions and pre-emption;
- manager/signature authority;
- company objects covering software, media, marketing, data, and permitted sports services;
- whether any sports/agent activity needs separate approval or entity structure;
- ITIDA registration/licensing implications;
- tax, VAT, payroll, and foreign-currency payments;
- personal-data controller/processor responsibilities;
- IP assignment and trademark registration;
- an employee/adviser equity plan;
- what investor-friendly structure is practical later.

Official starting points:

- GAFI electronic incorporation: https://gafi.gov.eg/en/contenttemplate/ca1b9d8b-76d1-46dc-a677-eb4ba5ccaae3
- ITIDA business setup information: https://www.itida.gov.eg/Arabic/Pages/Business-Egypt.aspx
- ITIDA contact/support: https://itida.gov.eg/English/Pages/ContactUs.aspx

## 16. Recommended founder conversation

Use this wording:

> I want us to protect both the company and our friendship. The idea and your football experience matter, and the product work also matters, but the company needs both of us to deliver for years. Let us work for eight weeks with clear responsibilities and no irreversible equity promise. If we both prove that we own essential halves of the business, we will agree a near-equal split with four-year vesting and put everything into a proper Egyptian founders agreement.
